How it works

One command, wired into your pipeline.

01

Add a project

weedout scan finds your lockfile, checks it, and prints what came back. Add --ci and it fails the build on anything critical or actively exploited.

No install to hand? Upload package.json, package-lock.json, requirements.txt or go.mod in the browser, or paste the contents. Either way the first scan runs immediately, not on the next schedule.

02

See the noise you're not being shown

The dashboard leads with the number of advisories that matched your dependencies and were filtered out. It's the largest number on the page, because it's the whole argument.

03

Check our work

Every filtered advisory stays on the Filtered tab with the reason attached — dev-only, transitive and unexploited, below threshold. Nothing is discarded, so you can disagree with a call.

04

Get one email, in plain language

Alerts explain what can go wrong, why you're seeing this one and not the other 200, and the exact command to fix it. One digest per scan, and never twice for the same finding.

What gets through

Three rules decide whether you hear from us.

They are the whole product, and they are written down so you can disagree with them. Every filtered advisory stays on your dashboard with the reason attached.

  • Exploited in the wild

    The CVE is in CISA's Known Exploited Vulnerabilities catalog. This overrides everything else — severity, dependency depth, even dev-only scope. If someone has a working exploit, "it's only in CI" is thin comfort.

  • Critical and shipping

    Critical severity in code that reaches production. No evidence of exploitation needed; critical findings in running code clear the bar on their own.

  • High and yours

    High severity in a package your project declares directly. Direct dependencies are the ones you can actually upgrade today, which is what makes them actionable rather than merely true.
