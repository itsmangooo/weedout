{# --------------------------------------------------------------------------- Usage --------------------------------------------------------------------------- #}

Three commands

Point it at your account, once.

weedout init writes a .weedout file holding your API key, and tells you to gitignore it. In CI, skip this entirely and set WEEDOUT_API_KEY instead — the environment always beats the file, so a key committed by accident can never quietly override the one your pipeline was configured with. 

$ weedout init
Wrote /home/you/acme/.weedout
Will scan package-lock.json

This file contains a credential. Add it to .gitignore.

Scan, and read what it did not tell you.

weedout scan finds your lockfile, uploads it, and prints the result. The number worth reading is the second one: how much matched your dependencies and was deliberately filtered out. That is the interruption you did not get. 

$ weedout scan
acme-storefront  package-lock.json
312 dependencies scanned · 47 filtered out as noise

  1 exploited  ·  2 critical  ·  5 high

  ! systeminformation@5.0.0  CVE-2021-21315  → 5.3.1
  • minimist@1.2.0  CVE-2021-44906  → 1.2.6

  https://weedout.dev/targets/42

Gate the pipeline on what is real.

weedout scan --ci exits 1 only for something critical or confirmed exploited in the wild. Everything else is reported and the build continues.

A failure to run — a rejected key, an unreachable service, no lockfile — exits 2, never 1. A pipeline that cannot tell an outage from a finding will eventually treat an expired key as a vulnerability, and somebody will fix that by deleting the step. 

$ weedout scan --ci
312 dependencies scanned · 47 filtered out as noise

  1 exploited  ·  2 critical

Failing: 3 finding(s) at critical severity or confirmed exploitation.
$ echo $?
1

Third-party packages

The whole CLI is the Go standard library: net/http for the request, encoding/json for the response, flag for the arguments, mime/multipart for the upload. Even the coloured output is about thirty lines of ANSI rather than a package.

That is not minimalism for its own sake. Every dependency in a security tool is something else you have to trust, and a CI runner is the last place that benefits from a dependency tree. There is nothing here for us to scan, which is the strongest version of this section we could show you.
